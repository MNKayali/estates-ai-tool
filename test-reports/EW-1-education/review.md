# Review — EW-1-education

Verdict: Solid report — all figures are internally consistent and the scope, risks and target-date verdict correctly reflect the answers, but it wrongly displays a specification level for a project type that shouldn't have one, the programme doesn't actually land construction inside a school holiday despite that being the client's core constraint, and one procurement claim is overstated.

## Issues

| # | Severity | Category | Page | Report says | Expected (scenario) | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Medium | Answers | 1, 3, 7 | "Specification: Standard" (cover); "External works only of 210 m² GIFA, standard specification, 7 priced scope items" (p3); "Rates are for a standard specification" (p7) | "No level-of-intervention or specification-level content anywhere (External works only asks neither)" | The questionnaire doesn't ask Q2.4 spec level for External works only, but the report shows a specification value on the cover, the Scope page and the cost page anyway. |
| 2 | Medium | Programme | 5, 8 | Construction (RIBA Stage 5) runs 7 Feb 2028 – 13 Mar 2028; Constraints Summary: "Works must be confined to the six-week summer holiday; any overrun into term time risks disruption" | Q3.5 "Term-time only working"; story says everything must happen in the six-week summer holiday | The scheduled construction window is February–March 2028 — term time, not a summer holiday — yet the report never schedules the work into a vacation window. It flags this as the top risk (R01, R03) rather than actually planning around it, so a reader could easily miss that the programme as printed doesn't fit the access constraint it names as the biggest risk. |
| 3 | Medium | Prose | 2 | "Direct Award — 3 Quotations is the required procurement route under formal competitive procurement rules for grant-funded projects, regardless of contract value." | AI must not present unsupported claims as settled fact | Stated as a categorical rule of law ("required... regardless of contract value") with no citation. This reads as an invented compliance claim a client could act on; the route itself is plausible, but the framing overstates certainty. |
| 4 | Low | Prose | 4 | R06: "Permitted development status assumed; prior-approval notification or university/crown land exemptions may apply" | — | "University/crown land exemptions" is generic boilerplate with no connection to a nursery school site; reads as an ungrounded addition. |
| 5 | Low | Cost | 6 | "1 nr trees" as the quantity/unit for Tree removal | — | Unit ("nr") and descriptor ("trees") are both printed, reading awkwardly ("1 nr trees" rather than "1 nr" or "1 tree"). |
| 6 | Low | Budget | 2 | No mention of budget anywhere in the executive summary | Q4.3 left blank — "the report should say so rather than invent a shortfall or surplus" | The report correctly avoids inventing a verdict, but never explicitly states that no budget figure was given, so a reader isn't told why there's no budget commentary. |
